Case Study: HYSA Savings Calculator

A consumer fintech tool that shows people exactly how much money they lose by keeping savings in a low-yield account. Built, tested, and iterated in under a week.

The problem

Most people with savings have never moved them to a HYSA. Not because they don't want better returns, but because nothing makes the cost feel real. Bankrate shows tables. NerdWallet shows percentages. Nobody shows you that $30k sitting in a checking account is costing you around $106 per month.

The target user was not a finance person. Just someone with money sitting somewhere, who never had a strong enough reason to act. I validated this by talking to five people in that situation before writing a single requirement. Every one of them said the same thing: they knew they should do something, they just never did. That was enough to move forward.

The goal was simple: show the dollar amount they are leaving behind and let them do something about it in under 30 seconds.

What I built and what I cut

The MVP had one job: show someone their number and give them a path to act. Everything else was V2. I cut user accounts, saved comparisons, and email rate alerts because none of them were needed to deliver that core moment. Adding them would have slowed the build and added friction before the tool had proven any value.

No signup. The value happens the moment someone sees their number. Adding a signup before that kills the whole point. Email capture only makes sense in V2 as a trade for rate alerts, not as a barrier.

Dropdown instead of bank tiles. The first version showed every bank as a tile. It looked complete, but pushed the calculator below the fold. Users are here to see one number. The bank choice is secondary. Switching to a dropdown cut vertical scroll by around 60%.

Coral instead of red. The first pass used bright red for loss amounts. It felt alarming. The goal was to create enough urgency to act, not to make people anxious. A softer coral kept the emotional signal without the panic.

Launch

I did not have a marketing budget or an audience. So I went where the target user already was: Reddit personal finance communities and a direct share on LinkedIn. The reasoning was simple. Reddit gave volume from people already thinking about money. LinkedIn gave reach to people who might share it further. No paid spend, just distribution to the right rooms.

247 unique sessions in the first two weeks confirmed the framing worked. People were staying long enough to engage and clicking through at a higher rate than the target.

What I got wrong

No feedback loop inside the tool. GA4 shows session time and bounce rate, but not where people got confused or dropped off. A simple star rating below the results with no signup would have given useful directional data.

40% of users picked "Traditional Bank 0.01%" as their rate, which meant most people do not actually know what their bank pays them. A bank search that surfaces their real rate would have made the tool significantly more useful.

What I would change at scale

Static rate data was the right call for an MVP. HYSA rates move slowly and a live API adds cost and complexity that was not justified yet. That decision changes once the user volume makes it worth it.

I built first and validated after. With more time I would have spent the first week talking to people about why they had not switched banks yet. That is where the real friction was, and understanding it earlier would have sharpened every other decision.

Why I built this

My background is B2B SaaS. Funding workflows, automation, systems that operations teams depend on daily. This was a different domain, but the same instinct: find where people are stuck, remove the friction, and make the right action obvious. Good product thinking does not stop at the job description.
